FOOD SAFETY, ALLERGEN MANAGEMENT & SAFE COOKING PRACTICES
===========================================================

SECTION 1: FOOD SAFETY FUNDAMENTALS

1.1 The Danger Zone
Bacteria multiply rapidly between 4°C/40°F and 60°C/140°F — this is called the "temperature danger zone."
Rule: never leave perishable food in the danger zone for more than 2 hours (1 hour if ambient temperature is above 32°C/90°F).
Refrigerator: maintain at or below 4°C/40°F.
Freezer: maintain at or below -18°C/0°F.
Hot holding: maintain food at or above 60°C/140°F.

1.2 Safe Internal Cooking Temperatures
The following temperatures must be reached at the thickest part, measured with a calibrated food thermometer:
- Poultry (chicken, turkey, duck): 74°C / 165°F — ALWAYS thoroughly cooked. Never consume undercooked poultry.
- Ground beef, pork, lamb: 71°C / 160°F — All ground/minced meats must be fully cooked.
- Beef, veal, lamb (whole cuts/steaks): 63°C / 145°F with 3-minute rest time.
- Pork (whole cuts): 63°C / 145°F with 3-minute rest time.
- Fish and seafood: 63°C / 145°F (flesh should be opaque and flake easily).
- Eggs and egg dishes: 71°C / 160°F (egg yolks and whites should be firm).
- Leftovers and casseroles: 74°C / 165°F.
WARNING: Raw or undercooked poultry, ground beef, and eggs carry significant risk of Salmonella, Campylobacter, and E. coli contamination. Always cook to safe temperatures.

1.3 Hand Washing
Wash hands for at least 20 seconds with soap and warm water:
- Before and after handling food.
- After handling raw meat, poultry, or seafood.
- After using the toilet.
- After touching your face, hair, or phone.
- After handling garbage.
Handwashing is the single most effective way to prevent foodborne illness.

1.4 Cross-Contamination Prevention
- Use separate cutting boards: one for raw meat/poultry/seafood, one for produce.
- Never place cooked food on a surface that held raw meat without thorough cleaning.
- Wash cutting boards, knives, and utensils with hot soapy water between uses.
- Store raw meat on the lowest shelf of the refrigerator to prevent dripping onto other foods.
- Never marinade and then use the same marinade as a sauce without boiling it first.

1.5 Proper Food Storage
Refrigerator storage guidelines:
- Raw poultry: 1-2 days.
- Raw ground meat: 1-2 days.
- Raw whole cuts of meat: 3-5 days.
- Cooked meats: 3-4 days.
- Cooked leftovers: 3-4 days.
- Fresh fish: 1-2 days.
- Eggs (in shell): 3-5 weeks.
- Hard cheese: 3-4 weeks after opening.
- Soft cheese (ricotta, cottage): 1 week.

SECTION 2: FOOD ALLERGEN MANAGEMENT

2.1 The 14 Major Allergens (as defined by UK/EU food law)
1. Cereals containing GLUTEN (wheat, rye, barley, oats, spelt, kamut)
2. CRUSTACEANS (shrimp, crab, lobster, crayfish)
3. EGGS
4. FISH
5. PEANUTS
6. SOYBEANS
7. MILK (and all dairy derivatives including lactose)
8. NUTS (almond, hazelnut, walnut, cashew, pecan, Brazil, pistachio, macadamia)
9. CELERY
10. MUSTARD
11. SESAME SEEDS
12. SULPHUR DIOXIDE & SULPHITES (wines, dried fruits)
13. LUPIN
14. MOLLUSCS (oysters, mussels, squid, clams)

2.2 Hidden Allergens — Common Traps
- GLUTEN: soy sauce (most brands), Worcestershire sauce, malt vinegar, beer, many spice mixes, some crisps.
- DAIRY: some margarines, non-dairy creamers (may still contain caseinate), some medications.
- EGGS: some pasta (fresh egg pasta), mayonnaise, hollandaise, many baked goods.
- SOY: many processed foods, some tuna cans (may contain soy broth), vegetarian meat alternatives.
- PEANUT: some Thai/African/Indonesian sauces, mixed nuts (cross-contamination), some cereals.
- SESAME: tahini, hummus, halva, many Asian sauces, some bread (sesame seed coating).
- FISH: Worcestershire sauce (contains anchovies), some Caesar dressings and pasta sauces.
Always read labels carefully. Ingredients can change without notice.

2.3 Anaphylaxis — Severe Allergy Emergency
Anaphylaxis is a severe, potentially life-threatening allergic reaction.
Symptoms: swelling of throat/tongue, difficulty breathing, severe rash, vomiting, drop in blood pressure, loss of consciousness.
Action: administer epinephrine (adrenaline) auto-injector (EpiPen) immediately and call emergency services.
For those cooking for others with severe allergies: always disclose all ingredients. When in doubt, do not serve.

2.4 Safe Cooking for Allergy Sufferers
- Prepare allergen-free dishes first, before other dishes, using freshly washed equipment.
- Use dedicated utensils and cookware for allergen-free cooking.
- When cooking for guests with allergies, ask about ALL medications, supplements, and secondary ingredients.
- Even trace amounts of allergens can trigger reactions in highly sensitive individuals.

SECTION 3: SPECIAL POPULATION CONSIDERATIONS

3.1 Pregnancy and Food Safety
Avoid during pregnancy:
- Raw or undercooked meat, poultry, and seafood.
- Raw or lightly cooked eggs.
- Unpasteurised milk, cheese, and juice.
- High-mercury fish: swordfish, shark, king mackerel, tilefish. Limit tuna consumption.
- Deli meats and hot dogs unless heated until steaming hot (risk of Listeria).
- Unpasteurised soft cheeses (Brie, Camembert, blue cheese).
- Raw sprouts (alfalfa, radish, mung bean) — cannot be made safe by washing.
Safe seafood choices: salmon, trout, cod, shrimp (cooked to 63°C), canned light tuna (limit 340g/week).

3.2 Cooking for Children
- Always cut grapes, cherry tomatoes, hot dogs, and firm vegetables lengthwise to prevent choking.
- Avoid honey for children under 12 months (botulism risk).
- Do not give whole nuts to children under 5.
- Cook eggs fully for children under 5, elderly, pregnant, or immunocompromised individuals.
- Avoid unpasteurised products.
- Reduce salt in recipes for infants and toddlers.

3.3 Cooking for Immunocompromised Individuals
Avoid: raw or undercooked meat, fish, eggs. Unpasteurised dairy. Raw sprouts. Deli meats (Listeria risk).
Always cook to recommended internal temperatures. Refrigerate leftovers within 1 hour.

SECTION 4: FOOD LABELLING AND READING LABELS

4.1 Understanding Food Labels
Ingredients are listed in descending order of weight (highest weight first).
Allergens must be highlighted (bold/underlined) in the ingredient list in most countries.
"May contain" or "made in a facility with" indicates cross-contamination risk — avoid if severely allergic.
"Gluten-free" claims in certified products mean <20 ppm gluten.
"Dairy-free" is not the same as "lactose-free" — some lactose-free products still contain milk protein (casein).

4.2 Use-By vs Best Before
USE BY: safety date — do not consume after this date even if it looks and smells fine. Applies to perishable, high-risk foods.
BEST BEFORE: quality date — food may still be safe after but quality declines. Applies to shelf-stable products.
SELL BY / DISPLAY UNTIL: for retailer use only, not a safety guideline for consumers.
When in doubt about food safety — throw it out.
