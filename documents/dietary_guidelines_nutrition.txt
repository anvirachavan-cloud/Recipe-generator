DIETARY GUIDELINES & NUTRITION FOR HEALTHY COOKING
===================================================
Based on Evidence-Based Nutritional Principles

SECTION 1: MACRONUTRIENTS

1.1 Carbohydrates
Carbohydrates are the body's primary energy source. Complex carbohydrates (whole grains, legumes, vegetables) are preferable to simple carbohydrates (sugar, white flour) because they digest slowly, preventing blood sugar spikes.
Recommended daily intake: 45-65% of total calories.
Good sources: quinoa, oats, sweet potato, brown rice, whole grain bread, lentils, chickpeas.
Foods to limit: refined sugar, white bread, sugary drinks, pastries.

1.2 Proteins
Proteins are essential for muscle repair, enzyme production, and immune function. Complete proteins (containing all 9 essential amino acids) include: eggs, meat, fish, dairy, quinoa, and soy.
Recommended: 0.8g per kg body weight (sedentary adults); 1.2-2.0g for athletes.
Vegetarian complete protein combinations: rice + lentils, corn + beans, pita + hummus.
Good sources: chicken breast, salmon, tofu, Greek yogurt, eggs, beans, lentils.

1.3 Fats
Dietary fats are essential for hormone production, vitamin absorption (A, D, E, K), and brain health.
Healthy fats (consume freely): avocado, olive oil, nuts, seeds, fatty fish.
Limit: saturated fats (butter, fatty meats, full-fat dairy).
Avoid: trans fats (hydrogenated oils, many processed foods).
Omega-3 fatty acids (anti-inflammatory): found in salmon, mackerel, flaxseed, chia seeds, walnuts.

SECTION 2: SPECIAL DIETS

2.1 Vegetarian Diet
A vegetarian diet excludes meat, poultry, and seafood. Well-planned vegetarian diets are nutritionally adequate for all life stages.
Key nutritional concerns:
- Vitamin B12: found only in animal products. Vegetarians should supplement or consume fortified foods (nutritional yeast, fortified milk).
- Iron: plant iron (non-heme) is less bioavailable. Enhance absorption by pairing with vitamin C. Sources: lentils, spinach, tofu, fortified cereals.
- Calcium: dairy, fortified plant milks, tofu (calcium-set), kale, bok choy.
- Zinc: whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds.
- Omega-3: flaxseed, chia seeds, walnuts, algae-based supplements.

2.2 Vegan Diet
A vegan diet excludes all animal products including honey and gelatin.
Additional concern beyond vegetarian: same as above, plus adequate calories (vegans often under-eat).
Protein sources: lentils (18g per cup cooked), tofu (20g per 100g), tempeh (19g per 100g), seitan (25g per 100g), black beans, chickpeas, edamame, quinoa.
Meal planning tip: aim for a protein source at every meal and include a vitamin C source to maximize iron absorption.

2.3 Ketogenic Diet
The ketogenic (keto) diet is a very low-carbohydrate, high-fat diet that shifts metabolism to ketosis.
Macronutrient ratios: Fat 70-75%, Protein 20-25%, Carbohydrates 5-10% (typically <50g net carbs/day).
Keto-friendly foods: meat, fatty fish, eggs, full-fat dairy, nuts, seeds, low-carb vegetables (leafy greens, cauliflower, broccoli, zucchini), avocado, olive oil, coconut oil.
Avoid: grains, sugar, most fruits, legumes, root vegetables, starchy vegetables.
Health considerations: may help manage type 2 diabetes and epilepsy. Consult a doctor before starting.
Not suitable for: pregnant women (without medical supervision), people with pancreatitis, liver disease, fat metabolism disorders.

2.4 Diabetic-Friendly Diet
Goal: maintain stable blood glucose levels.
Key principles:
- Choose low glycaemic index (GI) foods: oats, basmati rice, sweet potato, most vegetables, legumes.
- Distribute carbohydrates evenly throughout the day.
- Combine carbohydrates with protein, fat, or fibre to slow glucose absorption.
- Limit added sugars, refined carbohydrates, and sugary drinks.
- Recommended: Mediterranean-style diet pattern (olive oil, fish, vegetables, legumes, whole grains, limited red meat).
Foods to eat: leafy greens, whole grains, beans, fish, poultry (without skin), nuts, eggs, low-fat dairy.
Foods to limit: white bread, white rice, sugary cereals, candy, sweetened beverages, high-fat meats.

2.5 Gluten-Free Diet
Essential for people with coeliac disease or non-coeliac gluten sensitivity.
Gluten is found in: wheat (including spelt, kamut, farro), barley, rye, and sometimes oats (cross-contamination risk).
Safe grains: rice, corn, quinoa, buckwheat, sorghum, millet, amaranth, certified gluten-free oats.
Hidden sources of gluten: soy sauce (use tamari), many condiments, processed meats, some medications.
Cross-contamination: use separate utensils, cutting boards, and cookware for gluten-free cooking. Even crumbs matter.

SECTION 3: MICRONUTRIENTS IN COOKING

3.1 Preserving Nutrients When Cooking
Heat-sensitive vitamins: C and B-vitamins are destroyed by prolonged heat and water.
Best methods to preserve: steaming > microwaving > quick stir-fry > roasting > boiling.
Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K): better absorbed with dietary fat.
Cook tomatoes in olive oil to increase lycopene availability.
Raw vs cooked: most vegetables offer nutritional benefits both ways. Carrots, tomatoes, and spinach are more nutritious cooked; broccoli and garlic are more nutritious raw.

3.2 Antioxidant-Rich Foods
Anthocyanins (anti-inflammatory): blueberries, purple cabbage, red onion, black beans.
Lycopene: cooked tomatoes, watermelon, pink grapefruit.
Beta-carotene: carrots, sweet potato, pumpkin, mango.
Vitamin C: bell peppers, kiwi, citrus, broccoli, strawberries.
Flavonoids: dark chocolate (70%+), green tea, apples, onions.

SECTION 4: PORTION SIZES AND MEAL PLANNING

4.1 The Healthy Plate Method
Half the plate: non-starchy vegetables (broccoli, spinach, peppers, mushrooms, salad).
Quarter of the plate: lean protein (chicken, fish, legumes, tofu, eggs).
Quarter of the plate: complex carbohydrates (brown rice, quinoa, sweet potato, whole grain).
Side: small portion of healthy fat (olive oil dressing, handful of nuts, quarter avocado).
Drink: water as primary beverage.

4.2 Meal Prep Tips
Batch cook grains and legumes at the start of the week.
Roast a large tray of mixed vegetables for multiple meals.
Prepare a versatile protein base (e.g., roasted chicken, baked tofu, cooked lentils).
Store prepped ingredients separately; assemble meals to order for freshness.
Most cooked foods keep safely 3-4 days refrigerated at or below 4°C/40°F.
Label containers with contents and date prepared.
